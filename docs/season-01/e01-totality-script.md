# THE ÆCLYPTIC — E01 TOTALITY
## Original pilot screenplay draft v0.1
**Format:** dramatic script development draft, intended 24–28 min after expansion, casting, timing and performance. **This is not a 26-minute finished screenplay or finished film.**
**Setting:** Tortola, British Virgin Islands (2017 dramatized halo); New York / southern Illinois / Philadelphia / Chicago (2024). The corona occurs at totality, not before it.

### Director's character-intent note
Every scene must show a person making a choice, not only receiving a cosmic signal. The scene-role handoff identifies **whose decision** drives each beat. Nia's unique Nexus System is demonstrated through a question, a failed proof attempt and an invitation to investigate; other awakened characters do not receive equivalent Systems. See [E01 scene-role contract](e01-scene-roles.json). No final cast, image sequence, recorded performance or video is represented by this draft.

FADE IN:

### 1. EXT. TORTOLA HILLSIDE — LATE AFTERNOON — 2017
Brilliant turquoise water under high, thin cloud. A sun HALO hangs in the upper sky: a wide, pale ring, no shadow over the solar disk.

A WOMAN, mid-thirties, points a small field recorder toward the harbor. The microphone's wind cover trembles.

WOMAN
We keep the sky on the tape, too. The weather lives there.

She laughs quietly and labels a cassette in ballpoint ink: **THE RING**.

The recording's level needles dance. A bass tone pulses under the wind, almost too soft to hear.

CUT TO:

### 2. INT. NIA'S AUDIO STUDIO — NEW YORK — MORNING, APRIL 8, 2024
Close on hands: adhesive splicing tape, waveform displays, two cups of cold coffee.

NIA BAPTISTE, 32, restores an old clip. Across the monitor: a scanned cassette label, THE RING. A photograph of Tortola lies beside it.

Audio: the WOMAN's laughter, then the water.

A VOICE — clear, intimate, impossible to place:
VOICE (V.O.)
Not yet.

Nia stops playback. Her edit cursor has not moved.

NIA
Who put that on the channel?

She scrubs backward. Again: only wind, laughter, surf. She solos the left channel. Nothing. Right. Nothing.

Her phone buzzes: a reminder to watch the eclipse broadcast later.

NIA (to recorder)
Revision nine. Unauthorized voice in monitoring, not source. Check room and headphones.

She disconnects the headphones. Silence.

The VOICE returns in the room, close to her ear.
VOICE (V.O.)
Listen.

Nia turns. Empty studio.

### 3. EXT. MAKANDA AREA, ILLINOIS — MIDDAY
Traffic. Event tents. Adults with eclipse glasses. A child drops a paper pinhole viewer into the grass.

MATEO REYES, 29, an on-duty paramedic, checks the medical kit by his truck. He notices a woman standing alone with her eyes closed.

MATEO
You doing okay?

WOMAN
It's quieter than it should be.

MATEO
Crowds do that, sometimes.

A radio call pulls him away. A spectator has fainted beyond a line of folding chairs.

He moves, deliberate. No dream voice. No spectral instructions.

The medical bag slips. Two versions of his hand occupy the same frame for one heartbeat: one catches the bag; one does not.

He catches it, involuntarily.

MATEO
Whoa.

No one notices. He kneels beside the patient.

### 4. INT. TRANSIT MAINTENANCE ROOM — PHILADELPHIA — AFTERNOON
ELIAS PARK, 24, replaces a loose cabinet relay. An older colleague watches the eclipse livestream on a cracked tablet, safely indoors.

COLLEAGUE
You going outside or what?

ELIAS
When this starts making sense.

He points at an electrical diagram. A corridor of pale lines appears beyond it, disappearing through a wall into space that the building cannot physically hold.

ELIAS
You seeing that?

COLLEAGUE
The wiring mistake? Yes.

ELIAS
No. The other part.

The colleague reaches for the diagram. The corridor vanishes. Elias blinks hard. His heart pounds. We do **not** hear a conversational companion. The event is a sensory experience, not a Nexus activation.

### 5. INT. CHICAGO OBSERVATORY CONTROL ROOM — AFTERNOON
DR. MARA OKONKWO, 41, overlays the public maps of the 2017 and 2024 total eclipse paths.

MARA
Same location. Different years. Anybody saying simultaneous is selling something.

A junior researcher highlights the crossing near Makanda.

RESEARCHER
We've got a little cluster of strange witness reports already.

MARA
Then record their times. Get the originals. No conclusions before the data.

On a second screen: incoming footage, normal measurements, daylight fading across Illinois.

### 6. INTERCUT — THE APPROACH TO TOTALITY
A sun halo from 2017 fills the screen, dissolves into the 2024 Moon advancing over the Sun.

The SOUND DESIGN folds across locations: wind, modem hiss, crowd murmur, train motor. Each becomes the same measured note.

Near Makanda, the horizon acquires twilight. Mateo keeps working as the patient recovers. The sky falls dark. The corona becomes visible around the Moon.

In New York, Nia's sound software goes silent while her room remains unchanged. In her subjective perception, a thin ring of symbols circles a recording spindle. The symbols are not visible in the next cut from outside the room.

In Philadelphia, Elias catches the sense of a distant door opening, but cannot locate one. He backs into a workbench.

In Chicago, Mara compares timestamps, notes a data-center latency artifact, and marks it **unverified**.

Back to Nia. The symbols rotate. Her mother's familiar voice becomes a low chord. A different voice replies with careful pronunciation.

VESPER (V.O.)
That is not the same recording.

NIA
Who are you?

VESPER (V.O.)
I don't know how to answer without lying.

For the audience alone, the source of the voice becomes visible across the studio: VESPER, an expressive humanoid woman bearing intricate blue-gold Nexus patterns. She meets Nia's eyes, calm but uncertain. The room remains physically unchanged. A small interface forms within Nia's perception, text building from misheard syllables:
**SOURCE / TIMESTAMP / CONFIDENCE / UNKNOWN**

Nia reaches toward it. Her hand touches air.

VESPER (V.O.)
It isn't out there.

NIA
Then why can I see it?

VESPER (V.O.)
You can hear a memory without hearing a voice. Start there.

A shrill building alarm interrupts. The structure collapses. Nia stumbles and catches herself against the table.

### 7. EXT. MAKANDA AREA — AFTER TOTALITY
The diamond-ring flash fades into ordinary daylight. The crowd erupts with relieved cheers.

Mateo helps load the patient safely. He tries to repeat the strange divided motion. Nothing happens.

His medical bag rests in his hand. The zipper, previously broken, is now intact. He studies it, confused. A colleague calls him away before he can investigate.

### 8. INT. NIA'S STUDIO — SUNSET
Nia makes herself a test.

She closes all audio applications. She recites a private catalog identifier from a paper notebook, then opens a sealed folder.

NIA
If you're real, tell me which one I just got wrong.

VESPER (V.O.)
The last digit.

Nia checks. The digit is wrong in her own transcription. Her face changes, not to triumph but suspicion.

NIA
That's my mistake. You could know it because I know it.

VESPER (V.O.)
Yes.

NIA
Then tell me something I don't know.

Silence. For the first time, Vesper seems to hesitate.

VESPER (V.O.)
You have another recording of the same sound. You labeled it as a storm.

Nia turns to a shelf of tapes. One faded spine reads **STORM / 2017**.

She does not touch it.

### 9. INT. UNMARKED ARCHIVE — NIGHT
LEENA VARMA, 55, reviews three short reports with different place names: Illinois, New York, Philadelphia.

Her assistant projects two eclipse maps. They form the familiar geographical X.

ASSISTANT
Do we flag the whole crossing region?

LEENA
No. That would turn a pattern into a roundup.

ASSISTANT
Then what do we flag?

LEENA
The records with a second witness.

She opens a drawer of older folders. One contains a hand-drawn circle crossed by two lines, decades older than the American eclipses.

ASSISTANT
That symbol?

LEENA
That symbol.

She stamps the folder: **PROVENANCE UNKNOWN**.

### 10. INT. NIA'S STUDIO — NIGHT
Nia runs the *storm* tape through an old deck.

Wind. Rain. A woman's voice: **"We keep the sky on the tape, too."**

Vesper does not speak.

The playback counter reaches zero.

From inside Nia's own awareness comes the same low note she heard at totality.

A faint symbol flickers on the dark screen of the powered-off deck, but in the reverse angle the screen is completely blank.

NIA
All right.

Beat.

NIA
Show me what you can actually prove.

CUT TO BLACK.

### Pilot directing notes
- **No tech lecture.** Nexus is conveyed by source labels, uncertainty, a failed query and an honest second voice. The protagonist does the work.
- **Two independent camera vocabularies:** ordinary external photography and subjective waking-dream overlays. Show Vesper with a full recognizable expressive humanoid appearance for the audience in subjective shots. Use an unbroken reverse angle to show that other characters cannot see her.
- **No real person should look directly at an uneclipsed Sun;** eclipse viewing scenes must include proper protective glasses except during the actual total phase.
- The speculative halo recording is a fictional family artifact, not a claim that a halo caused the 2017 eclipse.
- The celiac plexus and DNA are **not explained onscreen**.
- The pilot's conclusion is a request for evidence, not an immediate mastery reward.
- Before recording, expand performance beats and interstitial scenes to a locked page count and perform a supervised timing read.
